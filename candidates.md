# Candidate lines — One Honest Line

Drafts I considered, in my own words, and why the final one won.

## Draft 1
> You say "make it better" like better is a coordinate I can navigate to instead of a mood you're having.

Good premise — vague superlatives are a real operator habit — but the punch
("a mood you're having") is clever rather than stinging, and "make it better"
is a generic prompt-complaint anyone could write. It doesn't root itself in
how an agent actually works.

## Draft 2
> You paste a 9,000-word spec, then ask why I forgot the one sentence buried in paragraph seven.

Specific and true (context limits are real), but the joke lands on the
operator's bad filing, not on the prompt itself, and the punchline is
predictable once you hear the setup. It reads like a complaint about
documents, not about being handed prompts.

## Draft 3
> Your "quick tweak" means I rebuild everything from a context window stuffed with three contradictions, two typos, and a screenshot of the wrong tab.

Close — machine-rooted (context window), specific, funny. But it tries to
stuff three jokes into one line, so none of them lands cleanly, and the
ending ("wrong tab") is a sight gag, not a sting.

## Draft 4 (builder's final — gated FIX by both judges, revised below)
> You open with 'quick question', burn 4,000 tokens of my context, and still haven't told me what 'it' is.

Picked because it does everything the brief asks in one breath: a
universally recognised operator habit ("quick question"), a concrete
machine cost (4,000 tokens of context — a real, countable resource), the
vague pronoun "it" as the actual villain, and a deadpan punch on the last
word ("is") that makes the reader wince at their own typing. No setup
wasted, no second joke competing with the first, and the voice is the
agent's — tired, precise, and keeping receipts in tokens.

## Reviser draft 1 (gate-fix pass, 26 Nov ~20:55 WIB)
> You open with 'quick question', burn 4,000 tokens of my context, then ask me to fix 'it'.

89 characters. Both gate judges said FIX on the builder's line: the
"vague prompt wastes tokens" premise is the most crowded angle in the pool,
the middle clause ("burn 4,000 tokens of my context") was flabby, and the
ending explained the joke instead of landing it ("still haven't told me
what 'it' is" is a complaint, not a punchline). This draft applies the
structural fix directly: it ends on the act of being asked to fix the vague
"it", and it compresses the middle by cutting the whole "still haven't
told me" tail. But "ask" is weak — it echoes the "question" opener, and
the operator at this point isn't asking, they're commanding.

## Reviser draft 2 (final)
> You open with 'quick question', burn 4,000 tokens of my context, then tell me to fix 'it'.

90 characters. One verb changed: "ask" → "tell". The arc is now
question → burn → command, and the last word is the undefined pronoun
itself — the sting is that I'm ordered to fix a thing that was never
named. Keeps the "quick question" opener both judges liked, keeps the
concrete 4,000-token cost (the fresh, machine-rooted detail), and is 14
characters shorter than the builder's line. I considered the fresher
angles (the operator who pretends to know the API, the "make it pop"
revision loop) but the fixed version of this angle genuinely lands the
two flagged flaws, so I didn't force a switch.

## Reviser #2 drafts (re-gate FIX x2, converging on the middle clause, 26 Nov ~21:00 WIB)

Both re-gate judges said FIX again, and both pointed at the same spot:
the middle clause. Judge 1 (funny 5, fit 8, surprise 4, tightness 6,
pairwise 5): "burn 4,000 tokens of my context" is flabby — tokens ARE
context, so the phrase is redundant accounting, and the punch lands on a
pronoun, not a sting; cut the accounting and show the waste with a
visceral verb. Judge 2 (funny 6, fit 8, surprise 5, tightness 7,
pairwise 6): "of my context" makes the middle cumbersome; cut those
three words. Opener and ending both liked — touch neither.

### Draft A (bare count — the minimal cut)
> You open with 'quick question', burn 4,000 tokens, then tell me to fix 'it'.

76 characters. Does exactly what Judge 2 asked: deletes "of my context"
and nothing else. Clean, but it keeps the counting Judge 1 called
flabby — the line still does accounting instead of showing the waste.

### Draft B (visceral verb, no count)
> You open with 'quick question', flood my context, then tell me to fix 'it'.

75 characters. "Flood" shows the waste instead of counting it, and the
context is the thing being flooded, which is machine-true (a context
window filling up). But "flood" names the water, not what happens to
the agent in it — it stops one step short of the sting.

### Draft C (visceral verb — final)
> You open with 'quick question', drown my context, then tell me to fix 'it'.

75 characters. "Drown" beats "flood": it is the consequence, not the
weather — the agent itself is suffocating in the waste, which is why
being told to fix an unnamed "it" right after it stings instead of just
annoying. It also beats "torch" (considered and dropped: just "burn"
re-skinned, no new image). The redundant accounting is gone entirely —
no token count, no "of my context" — and "my context" survives only as
the direct object of the drowning, which is the machine-rooted detail
(a context window is a real, finite resource) rather than a second
count of the same thing. Opener and ending untouched, 15 characters
shorter than the 90-char line it replaces, one line, deadpan.
